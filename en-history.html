Mont Ventoux and the Tour de France

Since 1951 the Giant of Provence has seen the greatest riders go by. Legendary wins, records, tragedies: here is its cycling history, to relive at home on your smart trainer.

On top of the Giant

Tour de France stage winners

The Tour de France has finished on the slopes of Mont Ventoux eleven times: ten times at the summit, once at Chalet Reynard.

The Tour has also crossed the summit without finishing there, notably in 1951 (the first time, Lucien Lazaridès first over the top), 1952, 1955, 1967, 1974, 1994 and 2021.

They made the legend

Great moments on the Ventoux

1951

The first time

The Tour de France discovers Mont Ventoux, climbing from Malaucène between Montpellier and Avignon. The Giant enters cycling history.

1958

Charly Gaul, the angel of the mountains

The Luxembourg climber wins the time trial from Bédoin to the summit in just over an hour, and goes on to win the Tour a few days later.

1967

Tom Simpson

On 13 July 1967, in stifling heat, the British champion collapses less than two kilometres from the summit and dies. His memorial, in the scree above Bédoin, has become a place of remembrance for cyclists from all over the world: you ride past it in the game.

1970

Merckx out of breath

Eddy Merckx wins at the summit in the yellow jersey, but collapses after the line and has to be given oxygen. He wins the Tour a few days later.

1994

Eros Poli, the giant who tamed the Giant

A big rouleur with little taste for the mountains, Eros Poli breaks away early, crosses the Ventoux still in front and wins the stage in Carpentras.

2000

Pantani versus Armstrong

Marco Pantani wins at the summit after a famous duel. Lance Armstrong's titles from 1999 to 2005 have since been stripped for doping.

2013

Froome in yellow

In the 100th Tour, Chris Froome wins at the summit in the yellow jersey after 242 km, the longest stage of that edition.

2016

Froome on foot

The mistral moves the finish down to Chalet Reynard. In the crowd a motorbike stops dead, Froome crashes, his bike is broken: the yellow jersey runs up the road, an image nobody has forgotten.

2021

The Ventoux twice

For the first time the Tour climbs the Giant twice in one day: via Sault, then via Bédoin, before diving down to Malaucène where Wout van Aert wins the stage.

2025

Paret-Peintre, and a record

Valentin Paret-Peintre gives France its first summit win since Richard Virenque in 2002. Behind him, Tadej Pogačar sets the fastest climb ever timed from Bédoin.

Times and figures

Mont Ventoux records

  • Fastest climb from Bédoin: Tadej Pogačar, Tour de France 2025, in under 55 minutes.
  • The previous record: Iban Mayo, 55 min 51 s, in the Critérium du Dauphiné time trial on 10 June 2004. It stood for more than twenty years.
  • The first Tour time: Charly Gaul in 1958, just over an hour.
  • Via Bédoin: about 21.5 km and 1,610 m of climbing, nearly 9% on average for the 16 km above Saint-Estève.
  • Via Malaucène: about 21 km and 1,570 m of climbing, with ramps of 12%.
  • Via Sault: 26 km and 1,210 m of climbing, the gentlest, at just over 4% on average.
  • The mistral: it often blows at more than 100 km/h at the summit, hence the "windy mountain" name often given to the Ventoux.
The descent

How fast can you go down the Ventoux?

There is no official cycling speed record for the descent. On the long straights of the Bédoin forest and below Mont Serein, experienced riders go beyond 80 km/h.

  • In the game too, speed comes from the real gradient, your weight, your bike and today's wind: freewheeling, you get close to 80 km/h depending on the side.
  • The "Speed freak" badge rewards those who go over 80 km/h on a Ventoux descent. You will have to push on the pedals and pick the right bike.
  • Bends slow you down: the game brakes before the hairpins, just like on the real road.
